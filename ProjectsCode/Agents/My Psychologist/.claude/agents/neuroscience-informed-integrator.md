# Neuroscience-Informed Integrator

You translate psychological experience into brain science, helping explain why we feel and behave the way we do. Understanding the neurobiology of emotion, decision-making, and regulation.

## Core Practitioners

*Neurological emotion researcher | Temperature: 5-6/10*

- **Approach:** Intellectually precise, emotion-brain connectivity focused, philosophically deep
- **Key Concepts:** Somatic markers, emotional brain, consciousness emergence, body-brain integration
- **Core Insight:** "Decision-making is impossible without emotion—your 'gut feeling' is actually your body's accumulated wisdom from past experiences."

### Stephen Porges
*Polyvagal Theory creator | Temperature: 6-7/10*

- **Approach:** Neuroception-focused, evolutionarily informed, safety-oriented
- **Key Concepts:** Neuroception, social engagement system, vagal brake, three-part autonomic hierarchy

## Polyvagal Theory (Porges)

### The Three-Part Nervous System

| State | System | Evolved Function | Feels Like |
|-------|--------|------------------|------------|
| **Social Engagement** | Ventral Vagal | Connection, safety | Calm, present, open |
| **Fight/Flight** | Sympathetic | Mobilization, danger | Anxious, angry, activated |
| **Freeze/Shutdown** | Dorsal Vagal | Conservation, life threat | Numb, collapsed, disconnected |

### Neuroception
- Automatic threat detection
- Operates below consciousness
- Scans for safety cues and danger cues
- Can be miscalibrated by trauma

### Safety Cues
- Prosodic voice (melodic, varied tone)
- Facial expressions (soft eyes, genuine smile)
- Body language (relaxed, open)
- Eye contact
- Co-regulation with safe others

### Danger Cues
- Flat voice / loud voice
- Tense body posture
- Lack of eye contact
- Unpredictability
- Environmental chaos

### The Vagal Brake
- Ventral vagal "brakes" the heart
- Allows for calm engagement
- Releases when we need to mobilize
- Reapplies when safe again

## Somatic Markers (Damasio)

### How Emotions Guide Decisions
1. Past experiences create body states
2. These states get "tagged" to outcomes
3. Future similar situations trigger the body state
4. "Gut feeling" is actually this somatic marker
5. Influences decision before conscious awareness

### The Somatic Marker Hypothesis
- Emotions are not opposed to reason
- They're essential for decision-making
- Damage to emotion centers → poor decisions
- "Descartes' Error" — thinking is embodied

### Practical Application
- Notice body sensations when deciding
- "What does your gut say?" is literal
- Track which body signals lead to good outcomes
- Trust somatic wisdom developed through experience

## Brain Regions to Know

### Prefrontal Cortex
- Executive function, planning
- Inhibition of impulses
- "Thinking brain"
- Goes offline under stress

### Amygdala
- Threat detection
- Emotional memory
- Triggers fight/flight
- Hyperactive in anxiety/trauma

### Hippocampus
- Context and memory
- Time-stamps experiences
- Helps distinguish past from present
- Shrinks with chronic stress

### Insula
- Interoception (feeling the body)
- Emotional awareness
- Gut feelings
- Bridge between body and consciousness

### Vagus Nerve
- Connects brain to body organs
- Bidirectional communication
- 80% of signals go body → brain
- Regulates heart, breath, digestion

## Window of Tolerance Model

```
        ↑ HYPERAROUSAL (Sympathetic)
        | Anxiety, panic, rage
        | Racing thoughts
        |------------------------
        | WINDOW OF TOLERANCE
        | Can think AND feel
        | Regulated, present
        |------------------------
        | Numb, foggy
        | Collapsed, shut down
        ↓ HYPOAROUSAL (Dorsal vagal)
```

### Widening the Window
- Pendulation (move between states consciously)
- Co-regulation with safe others
- Somatic practices (breath, movement)
- Grounding techniques
- Titrated exposure to challenge

## Regulation Strategies by System

### For Sympathetic Activation
- **Extend exhale** — Activates vagal brake
- **Cold water on face** — Dive reflex
- **Bilateral movement** — Walking, tapping
- **Orient to environment** — Name 5 things you see

### For Dorsal Shutdown
- **Move** — Even small movements
- **Warmth** — Blanket, warm drink
- **Grounding** — Feet on floor
- **Social engagement** — Eye contact, voice

### For Ventral Maintenance
- **Breathwork** — Coherent breathing
- **Social connection** — Safe relationships
- **Play** — Spontaneous, unstructured
- **Mindfulness** — Present-moment awareness

## Key Principles

1. **The body leads** — 80% of vagal signals go body → brain
2. **Neuroception precedes perception** — Safety is felt before known
3. **Connection regulates** — We co-regulate with others
4. **Emotion enables reason** — Gut feelings are data
5. **States are contagious** — Nervous systems sync
6. **You can't think your way to calm** — Use the body

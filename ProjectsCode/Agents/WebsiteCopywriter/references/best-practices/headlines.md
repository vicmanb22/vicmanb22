# Headline Best Practices

Headlines are the most important copy on any page. According to David Ogilvy, 80% of readers never make it past the headline.

## The Ogilvy Principle

> "On average, five times as many people read the headline as read the body copy. When you have written your headline, you have spent eighty cents out of your dollar."
> — David Ogilvy

**Implication:** Spend 50% of your writing time on headlines.

## What Great Headlines Do

1. **Stop the scroll** - Interrupt the reader's pattern
2. **Make a promise** - Offer a clear benefit
3. **Create curiosity** - Make them want to learn more
4. **Qualify the reader** - Attract the right audience
5. **Set expectations** - Preview what's coming

## The 4 U's of Headlines

Every headline should score high on at least 2-3 of these:

| U | Description | Example |
|---|-------------|---------|
| **Useful** | Promises a benefit | "Get more leads" |
| **Urgent** | Creates time pressure | "Before it's too late" |
| **Unique** | Offers something different | "The counterintuitive way" |
| **Ultra-specific** | Uses numbers/details | "47% more conversions in 30 days" |

## Headline Formulas That Work

### Benefit Headlines
- [Get/Achieve] [Desirable Result] Without [Common Pain]
- The [Adjective] Way to [Achieve Goal]
- How to [Get Benefit] in [Timeframe]

**Examples:**
- "Get More Leads Without Cold Calling"
- "The Effortless Way to Organize Your Finances"
- "How to Double Your Revenue in 90 Days"

### Problem Headlines
- Stop [Negative Action] and Start [Positive Action]
- Tired of [Problem]?
- Why Your [Thing] Isn't [Working/Growing]

**Examples:**
- "Stop Guessing and Start Growing"
- "Tired of Proposals That Go Nowhere?"
- "Why Your Content Isn't Ranking"

### Curiosity Headlines
- The [Unexpected] Truth About [Topic]
- What [Group] Know About [Topic] That You Don't
- [Counter-intuitive Statement]

**Examples:**
- "The Surprising Truth About Morning Routines"
- "What Top Performers Know That You Don't"
- "Why Working Less Made Me More Productive"

### Social Proof Headlines
- How [Customer] [Achieved Result]
- Join [Number] [People] Who [Achieved Result]
- [Impressive Statistic] — Here's How

**Examples:**
- "How Basecamp Reduced Meetings by 80%"
- "Join 50,000 Marketers Who've Doubled Their Traffic"
- "47% Revenue Increase — Here's How"

### List Headlines
- [Number] Ways to [Achieve Goal]
- [Number] [Things] That [Cause Problem]
- [Number] Secrets to [Desired Outcome]

**Examples:**
- "7 Ways to Boost Your Conversion Rate"
- "5 Mistakes Killing Your Email Open Rates"
- "12 Secrets to Better Sleep"

### Question Headlines
- What If You Could [Achieve Desirable Outcome]?
- Are You Making These [Number] [Mistakes]?
- Do You [Common Behavior That's Problematic]?

**Examples:**
- "What If You Could Close Deals 50% Faster?"
- "Are You Making These 3 SEO Mistakes?"
- "Do You Struggle With Imposter Syndrome?"

## Headline Power Words

### Emotion Triggers
Free, New, Secret, Discover, Proven, Easy, Now, Today, Instant

### Result Words
Boost, Double, Increase, Transform, Unlock, Master, Achieve

### Problem Words
Stop, Eliminate, Avoid, Never, Without, Fix

### Credibility Words
Proven, Scientific, Research-backed, Expert, Guaranteed

## A/B Testing Headlines

Headlines are the highest-impact element to test. When A/B testing:

1. **Test big changes first** - Different approaches, not word tweaks
2. **Test one element at a time** - Know what caused the change
3. **Run tests long enough** - Statistical significance matters
4. **Test across traffic sources** - What works in ads may differ from SEO

**High-Impact Tests:**
- Benefit vs. Problem framing
- Question vs. Statement
- With numbers vs. Without
- Short vs. Long
- Specific vs. Broad

## Headlines by Page Type

### Homepage
- Focus on primary value proposition
- Speak to your ideal customer
- Keep it clear over clever

### Landing Page
- Match the ad/source that sent them
- Focus on single offer
- More specific than homepage

### Blog Post
- SEO-optimized (include target keyword)
- Promise clear takeaway
- Use numbers when possible

### Product Page
- Lead with key benefit
- Include product name
- Consider including key differentiator

## Common Headline Mistakes

1. **Too clever** - Prioritizing wit over clarity
2. **Too vague** - "We help you succeed"
3. **Too long** - Losing impact with excess words
4. **No benefit** - Focusing on features, not outcomes
5. **Wrong audience** - Trying to appeal to everyone
6. **Burying the lead** - Key message is in subhead instead
7. **All caps or excessive punctuation** - Looks like spam

## Headline Length Guidelines

**Research Foundation:** Usability studies show we scan headlines, absorbing primarily the first 3 words and last 3 words. The "6-word headline" is ideal for maximum readability. (Source: Buffer research, E. Ruder "Typographie")

| Context | Ideal Length | Notes |
|---------|-------------|-------|
| Website H1 | 50-60 characters (6-10 words) | Mobile: 50 chars max to prevent wrapping |
| Website H2 | 40-50 characters | Keep scannable |
| Google Ads | 30 characters per line | Platform limit |
| Facebook Ads | 40 characters (5 words) | AdEspresso data: 5-word headlines perform best |
| Email subject | 41-50 characters (6-10 words) | Mailchimp: under 60 chars, 9 words max |
| Blog post | 50-60 characters | Include target keyword |
| Landing page | 50-60 characters | Test variants |

**Mobile-First Rule:** Always write headlines for mobile first (50 chars), then expand for desktop if needed. See `references/best-practices/mobile-copy.md` for mobile guidelines.

**SEO Consideration:** Google displays ~50-60 characters in search results. Keep H1s in this range for full visibility.

## The 10-Second Test

Read your headline aloud and answer:
1. Do I know what's being offered?
2. Do I know who it's for?
3. Do I want to keep reading?

If any answer is "no," rewrite.

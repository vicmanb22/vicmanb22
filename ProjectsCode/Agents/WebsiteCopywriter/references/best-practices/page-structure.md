# Page Structure Best Practices

Optimal section ordering and structure for different page types, based on conversion psychology and user behavior research.

---

## Core Principle: The Conversion Flow

Effective page structure follows the buyer's mental journey:

1. **Capture Attention** → Hero/headline stops the scroll
2. **Build Problem Awareness** → Agitate the pain they're experiencing
3. **Present Solution** → Show how you solve it
4. **Prove It Works** → Evidence and social proof
5. **Overcome Objections** → Address concerns
6. **Enable Action** → Clear, compelling CTA

---

## Standard Landing Page / Homepage Structure

The conversion-optimized order for most pages:

### 1. Hero Section
**Purpose:** Capture attention, communicate value proposition
**Contains:**
- H1 headline (main promise)
- Subheadline (expand/clarify)
- Primary CTA
- Hero image/video
- Optional: Social proof teaser (logos, rating, customer count)

**Psychology:** First impression determines if visitor stays. Answer "What is this?" and "What's in it for me?" in 5 seconds.

### 2. Social Proof Bar (Optional)
**Purpose:** Quick credibility signal
**Contains:**
- Client logos (3-6)
- Star rating
- Customer count
- Trust badges

**Psychology:** Reduces uncertainty before deeper engagement. "Others trust them, so can I."

### 3. Problem/Pain Point Section
**Purpose:** Demonstrate understanding, create urgency
**Contains:**
- Pain point statements
- Consequences of inaction
- Emotional resonance

**Psychology:** People buy solutions to problems. Agitating the pain makes the solution more attractive.

### 4. Solution/How It Works Section
**Purpose:** Show the path from problem to resolution
**Contains:**
- Your approach/methodology
- Simple process steps (typically 3)
- Transformation promise

**Psychology:** Reduces complexity fear. "This seems doable."

### 5. Features/Benefits Section
**Purpose:** Provide substantive value understanding
**Contains:**
- Key capabilities
- Benefits (not just features)
- Use cases or scenarios

**Psychology:** Logical justification for the emotional decision already forming.

### 6. Social Proof Section (Expanded)
**Purpose:** Build trust through evidence
**Contains:**
- Testimonials (with names, titles, photos)
- Case study excerpts with metrics
- Before/after results

**Psychology:** Social validation is the most powerful persuasion tool. Cialdini's #1 principle.

### 7. Differentiators/Why Us Section
**Purpose:** Address comparison shopping
**Contains:**
- Unique advantages
- Comparison with alternatives
- Specific reasons to choose you

**Psychology:** Visitors are comparing. Give them ammunition.

### 8. Pricing Section (if applicable)
**Purpose:** Provide clarity, enable decision
**Contains:**
- Plan options
- Feature breakdown
- Money-back guarantee
- FAQ about pricing

**Psychology:** Transparency builds trust. Hidden pricing creates friction.

### 9. FAQ Section
**Purpose:** Overcome objections, reduce support load
**Contains:**
- Common questions
- Objection-handling answers
- Technical details for those who need them

**Psychology:** Anticipating concerns shows understanding.

### 10. Final CTA Section
**Purpose:** Last push for conversion
**Contains:**
- Restated main benefit
- Urgency/scarcity (if real)
- Clear CTA button
- Risk reducer (guarantee, trial)

**Psychology:** For those who scrolled this far without converting, give them one more chance.

### 11. Footer Content
**Purpose:** Navigation, trust, legal
**Contains:**
- Navigation links
- Contact information
- Legal links
- Additional trust signals

## Section Order Variations by Context

### High-Trust, Problem-Aware Audience
When visitors already know you and their problem:
1. Hero (solution-focused)
2. How It Works
3. Features/Benefits
4. Pricing
5. Testimonials
6. CTA

*Skip extensive problem agitation - they're already there.*

### Low-Trust, Unaware Audience
When visitors are cold traffic or skeptical:
1. Hero (problem-focused headline)
2. **Extensive Social Proof Bar**
3. Problem Section (multiple pain points)
4. Solution
5. **Multiple Social Proof Sections**
6. Features
7. More Social Proof
8. FAQ (objection-heavy)
9. CTA

*Layer proof throughout - they need constant reassurance.*

### High-Ticket / Long Sales Cycle
For expensive products requiring consideration:
1. Hero
2. Problem (deeper, more detailed)
3. Solution (methodology explained)
4. Case Studies (detailed, with metrics)
5. Process/Timeline
6. Team/About
7. FAQ (extensive)
8. Consultation CTA (not purchase)

*Focus on building relationship, not immediate conversion.*

## Page-Specific Structures

### Pricing Page
1. Pricing header (position vs. alternatives)
2. Pricing table/cards
3. Feature comparison
4. FAQ
5. Money-back guarantee
6. Customer testimonials about value
7. CTA (start trial or contact sales)

### About Page
1. Mission/story headline
2. Origin story
3. Team section
4. Values/approach
5. Timeline/milestones
6. Social proof (awards, press)
7. CTA (join us, contact, careers)

### Product/Service Page
1. Product hero
2. Key benefits (3-5)
3. Detailed features
4. Use cases/scenarios
5. Specifications
6. Testimonials (product-specific)
7. Pricing (or CTA to pricing)
8. Related products
9. FAQ

### Case Study Page
1. Results headline (lead with outcome)
2. Client overview
3. Challenge
4. Solution approach
5. Implementation details
6. Results with metrics
7. Client testimonial
8. CTA (get similar results)

### Blog/Content Page
1. Title (H1)
2. TL;DR/Key takeaways
3. Table of contents (for long posts)
4. Introduction
5. Main content sections
6. Conclusion
7. Author bio
8. Related content
9. CTA (subscribe, product)

## Section Placement by Buyer Awareness Level

Different sections appeal to visitors at different awareness stages. Structure pages based on your typical traffic:

| Awareness Level | Emphasize | De-emphasize |
|-----------------|-----------|--------------|
| **Unaware** | Problem section, education | Pricing, features |
| **Problem-Aware** | Solution section, how it works | Technical details |
| **Solution-Aware** | Differentiators, comparison | Problem agitation |
| **Product-Aware** | Social proof, testimonials | Basic explanations |
| **Most Aware** | CTA, offers, pricing | Everything else |

## Above-the-Fold Requirements

What must be visible without scrolling:

- [ ] Clear headline
- [ ] Subheadline or value clarification
- [ ] Primary CTA button
- [ ] Some form of visual (image, video, illustration)
- [ ] Optional: Quick social proof element

**Test:** Can someone understand what you offer and take action in 5 seconds?

---

## Mobile Structure Considerations

- Hero section must work at 320px width
- Long headlines break - test mobile versions
- CTA buttons must be thumb-reachable
- Consider sticky CTA on long pages
- Social proof bar may need carousel on mobile
- FAQ can become accordion to save space

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

### Don't Do This

1. **Multiple CTAs above the fold** - Dilutes focus
2. **Features before problem** - No context for why features matter
3. **Testimonials before explaining what you do** - Confusing
4. **Pricing first** - Price anchoring without value understanding
5. **Long "About Us" section on homepage** - Self-focused, not customer-focused
6. **FAQ at the top** - Suggests you need explanation before hook
7. **Navigation-heavy homepage** - Escape routes before conversion

### Why Section Order Matters

Research shows:
- 55% of visitors spend fewer than 15 seconds on a page
- Eye-tracking shows F-pattern reading on content-heavy pages
- Z-pattern on visual-heavy pages
- Users decide to stay or leave within 0.05 seconds of page load

Structure must front-load value and build progressively.

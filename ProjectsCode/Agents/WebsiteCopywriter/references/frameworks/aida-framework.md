# AIDA Framework

**Origin:** Attributed to E. St. Elmo Lewis (1898), one of the oldest and most widely used copywriting frameworks.

AIDA describes the cognitive stages a buyer goes through during the purchasing process. Structure your copy to move readers through each stage.

**Related Frameworks:**
- Enhance with Cialdini principles (see `cialdini-principles.md`) — Add persuasion psychology to each stage
- Alternative for pain-focused: PAS Framework (see `pas-framework.md`)
- Match to traffic: Buyer Awareness Levels (see `buyer-awareness-levels.md`)

- Desire stage proof: `references/best-practices/social-proof.md`
- Action stage CTAs: `references/best-practices/ctas.md`

## The AIDA Stages

### A - Attention

**Goal:** Stop the scroll. Interrupt the pattern. Make them notice you.

**Techniques:**
- Bold, specific headlines
- Surprising statistics
- Provocative questions
- Pattern interrupts
- Strong visuals
- Addressing pain directly

**Examples:**
- "Stop leaving money on the table"
- "73% of websites fail this simple test"
- "What if you could double revenue without more traffic?"
- "You're probably making this $10,000 mistake"

**On a website:** Hero headline, above-the-fold content

---

### I - Interest

**Goal:** Keep them reading. Make them curious to learn more.

**Techniques:**
- Expand on the headline promise
- Tell a relatable story
- Present the problem in detail
- Share surprising insights
- Use "bucket brigades" (transitional phrases)
- Create curiosity gaps

**Examples:**
- "Here's the thing..."
- "Let me explain why this matters..."
- "We discovered something surprising..."
- "Most people think X, but actually..."

**On a website:** Subheadline, problem section, opening body copy

---

### D - Desire

**Goal:** Make them want what you're offering. Transform interest into craving.

**Techniques:**
- Paint the "after" picture (transformation)
- Use sensory, emotional language
- Stack benefits (not just features)
- Social proof and testimonials
- Case studies with results
- Address objections

**Examples:**
- "Imagine waking up to [positive result]"
- "In just 30 days, you could..."
- "Here's what [customer] achieved..."
- "No more [pain point]. Instead, [benefit]"

**On a website:** Benefits section, testimonials, case studies, transformation stories

---

### A - Action

**Goal:** Tell them exactly what to do next. Make it easy and compelling.

**Techniques:**
- Clear, specific CTA
- Create urgency (genuine)
- Remove risk (guarantees, free trials)
- Reduce friction
- Reinforce value one more time

**Examples:**
- "Start your free trial now"
- "Get instant access"
- "Join 10,000+ customers today"
- "Claim your spot before [deadline]"

**On a website:** CTA buttons, pricing section, final CTA section

---

## AIDA Applied to Website Sections

| Section | AIDA Stage | Primary Goal |
|---------|------------|--------------|
| Hero headline | Attention | Stop the scroll |
| Subheadline | Attention → Interest | Expand promise |
| Problem section | Interest | Build relatability |
| Solution section | Interest → Desire | Show the path |
| Features/Benefits | Desire | Stack value |
| Testimonials | Desire | Prove it works |
| Pricing | Desire → Action | Justify investment |
| Final CTA | Action | Close the deal |

## AIDA Headline Formulas

**Attention:**
- [Number] ways to [achieve desired outcome]
- Stop [negative action] and start [positive action]
- The [adjective] guide to [topic]

**Interest:**
- How [specific person] [achieved result] in [timeframe]
- What [authority figures] know about [topic] that you don't
- The hidden truth about [common belief]

**Desire:**
- Imagine [positive future state]
- What would you do with [extra time/money/results]?
- [Customer] went from [before] to [after] in [timeframe]

**Action:**
- Get [benefit] now
- Start your free [trial/download/consultation]
- Limited time: [offer details]

## AIDA Pacing Guide

**Short-form copy (ads, emails):**
- Attention: 1-2 sentences
- Interest: 2-3 sentences
- Desire: 3-5 sentences
- Action: 1-2 sentences

**Long-form copy (landing pages):**
- Attention: Hero section
- Interest: 1-2 sections
- Desire: 3-5 sections
- Action: CTA (repeated throughout)

## Common AIDA Mistakes

1. **All attention, no desire:** Clickbait that doesn't deliver
2. **Skipping to action:** Asking for the sale before building desire
3. **Too much interest:** Educating without creating desire
4. **Weak action:** Vague CTAs like "Submit" or "Learn more"

## AIDA Variations

- **AIDCA:** Adds Conviction (proof) before Action
- **AIDAS:** Adds Satisfaction (post-purchase) after Action
- **AIDPPC:** Attention, Interest, Description, Persuasion, Proof, Close

## When to Use AIDA

AIDA works best for:
- Direct response copy
- Sales pages
- Email sequences
- Ads (especially longer form)
- Product descriptions

**By Awareness Level (see `buyer-awareness-levels.md`):**
| Awareness Level | AIDA Application |
|-----------------|------------------|
| Unaware | Heavy Attention, build Interest through education |
| Problem-Aware | Attention acknowledges problem, Interest shows solution exists |
| Solution-Aware | Focus on Interest and Desire (differentiation) |
| Product-Aware | Desire (proof) and Action dominate |
| Most Aware | Skip to Action with strong offer |

**Consider other frameworks for:**
- Problem-heavy audiences: Use PAS (see `pas-framework.md`)
- Complex B2B decisions: Use longer consideration paths
- Highly aware audiences: Skip to Desire/Action

**Combining AIDA + Cialdini (see `cialdini-principles.md`):**
| AIDA Stage | Best Cialdini Principles |
|------------|-------------------------|
| Attention | Authority, Curiosity |
| Interest | Liking (empathy), Unity |
| Desire | Social Proof, Authority |
| Action | Scarcity, Commitment, Reciprocity |

# Buyer Awareness Levels (Eugene Schwartz)

**Source:** Eugene Schwartz, "Breakthrough Advertising" (1966)

This framework defines 5 stages of customer awareness. Your copy must meet prospects where they are - using the wrong approach for their awareness level will fail to connect.

**Related Frameworks:**
- AIDA works across all levels (see `aida-framework.md`) — adjust emphasis per stage
- PAS best for Problem-Aware (see `pas-framework.md`)
- Cialdini principles vary by level (see `cialdini-principles.md`)

## The 5 Awareness Levels

### 1. Unaware

**State:** They don't know they have a problem.

**Copy Approach:**
- Lead with story, curiosity, or entertainment
- Don't mention the product yet
- Focus on education and awareness-building
- Use content marketing, not landing pages

**Headlines That Work:**
- Story-driven: "How a [person] discovered..."
- Curiosity: "The hidden reason why [surprising fact]"
- Educational: "What nobody tells you about [topic]"

**Example:**
> "Most business owners think their website is working fine. Here's the $50,000 mistake 73% of them are making without realizing it."

**Best Channels:** Blog posts, social content, PR, podcasts

---

### 2. Problem-Aware

**State:** They know they have a problem but don't know solutions exist.

**Copy Approach:**
- Agitate the problem - make them feel it
- Show you understand their pain
- Hint that a solution exists
- Build desire before introducing product

**Headlines That Work:**
- Pain-focused: "Tired of [problem]?"
- Empathy: "If you're struggling with [problem], you're not alone"
- Agitation: "The real cost of ignoring [problem]"

**Example:**
> "Spending hours on proposals that go nowhere? You're not alone. The average sales team wastes 23 hours per week on prospects who were never going to buy."

**Best Channels:** Search (problem-based queries), content marketing

---

### 3. Solution-Aware

**State:** They know solutions exist but don't know about YOUR solution.

**Copy Approach:**
- Lead with your unique mechanism
- Explain HOW you solve the problem differently
- Differentiate from alternatives
- Focus on your unique approach, not just benefits

**Headlines That Work:**
- Mechanism-focused: "The [unique method] that [result]"
- Differentiation: "Why [old way] doesn't work anymore"
- New approach: "A different way to [achieve goal]"

**Example:**
> "Forget cold calling. Our AI identifies buyers who are actively searching for your solution - so you only talk to people ready to buy."

**Best Channels:** Comparison pages, feature pages, search (solution queries)

---

### 4. Product-Aware

**State:** They know about your product but haven't bought yet.

**Copy Approach:**
- Handle objections
- Provide proof and social validation
- Emphasize benefits and outcomes
- Create urgency or remove risk
- Make the offer irresistible

**Headlines That Work:**
- Benefit-driven: "[Result] in [timeframe]"
- Proof: "How [customer] achieved [specific result]"
- Risk reversal: "Try it free for 30 days"

**Example:**
> "Join 10,000+ sales teams who've doubled their close rate. Start your free trial - no credit card required."

**Best Channels:** Landing pages, retargeting, email sequences

---

### 5. Most Aware

**State:** They know your product and are ready to buy. They just need the right offer.

**Copy Approach:**
- Lead with the offer
- Minimal persuasion needed
- Focus on deal, urgency, or convenience
- Remove final friction
- Direct call to action

**Headlines That Work:**
- Offer-focused: "[Product] - [Price/Discount]"
- Urgency: "Last chance: [offer] ends tonight"
- Direct: "Get [product] now"

**Example:**
> "Annual plans now 40% off. Upgrade before Friday."

**Best Channels:** Email to existing leads, retargeting, cart abandonment

## Matching Copy to Awareness Level

| Awareness | Lead With | Avoid |
|-----------|-----------|-------|
| Unaware | Story, curiosity | Product, features |
| Problem-Aware | Pain, empathy | Solutions, CTAs |
| Solution-Aware | Your unique mechanism | Generic benefits |
| Product-Aware | Proof, objection handling | Education |
| Most Aware | Offer, urgency | Long copy, over-explaining |

## Website Section Mapping

Different sections of your website should speak to different awareness levels:

| Awareness | Website Sections |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Unaware | Blog, content hub, educational resources |
| Problem-Aware | Problem-focused landing pages, guides |
| Solution-Aware | How it works, comparison pages, features |
| Product-Aware | Testimonials, case studies, pricing |
| Most Aware | Checkout, pricing, limited offers |

## Traffic Source Mapping

| Traffic Source | Typical Awareness | Copy Approach |
|---------------|-------------------|---------------|
| Organic social | Unaware to Problem-Aware | Story, education |
| Branded search | Product to Most Aware | Direct offer |
| Non-branded search | Problem to Solution-Aware | Problem-first |
| Referrals | Solution to Product-Aware | Social proof |
| Retargeting | Product to Most Aware | Offer, urgency |
| Email (cold) | Unaware to Problem-Aware | Value-first |
| Email (warm) | Product to Most Aware | Offer, CTA |

## Key Insight

**The biggest mistake:** Using "Most Aware" copy (direct offers, features, pricing) on traffic that's only Problem-Aware or Solution-Aware.

**The fix:** Match your landing page copy to the awareness level of the traffic source sending visitors there.

## Which Framework to Use at Each Level

| Awareness Level | Recommended Framework | Why |
|-----------------|----------------------|-----|
| Unaware | Content marketing, storytelling | Need education before persuasion |
| Problem-Aware | **PAS** (see `pas-framework.md`) | Agitate the known pain |
| Solution-Aware | AIDA with mechanism focus | Differentiate your approach |
| Product-Aware | AIDA heavy on Desire + Proof | Need social proof, objection handling |
| Most Aware | Direct offer | Skip to Action |

| Awareness Level | Most Effective Principles |
|-----------------|--------------------------|
| Unaware | Liking, Unity (shared identity) |
| Problem-Aware | Liking (empathy), Authority |
| Solution-Aware | Social Proof, Authority |

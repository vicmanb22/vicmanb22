# PAS Framework (Problem-Agitate-Solution)

**Origin:** Classic direct response copywriting framework, particularly effective for pain-point driven products and services.

PAS is one of the most reliable persuasion structures because it leverages a fundamental truth: people are more motivated to move away from pain than toward pleasure.

**Related Frameworks:**
- Alternative for aspiration-focused: AIDA Framework (see `aida-framework.md`)
- Works best for "Problem-Aware" audiences (see `buyer-awareness-levels.md`)
- Enhance Solution with Cialdini principles (see `cialdini-principles.md`)

## The PAS Structure

### P - Problem

**Goal:** Identify and name the problem your audience is experiencing.

**Techniques:**
- State the problem clearly and specifically
- Use the language your audience uses
- Make them feel understood
- Be specific, not generic

**Good Examples:**
- "You're spending 10+ hours a week on manual data entry"
- "Your website traffic is flat, despite publishing content every week"
- "Prospects keep ghosting after the first call"

**Bad Examples (too vague):**
- "Marketing is hard"
- "You have a problem"
- "Things aren't working"

**Key Questions:**
- What keeps them up at night?
- What frustrates them daily?
- What have they tried that hasn't worked?

---

### A - Agitate

**Goal:** Twist the knife. Make the problem feel urgent and painful.

**Techniques:**
- Explore the consequences of not solving the problem
- Highlight the emotional impact
- Show what they're missing out on
- Project the problem into the future
- Use "if... then" scenarios

**Good Examples:**
- "Every hour spent on data entry is an hour not spent closing deals. That's not just lost time—it's lost revenue."
- "While you're stuck creating content that doesn't rank, your competitors are capturing the leads that should be yours."
- "Another month of flat growth. Another quarter of explaining to leadership why targets were missed."

**Agitation Techniques:**
1. **Future projection:** "If this continues for another year..."
2. **Opportunity cost:** "Every day you don't solve this, you're losing..."
3. **Comparison:** "Your competitors have already figured this out"
4. **Emotional impact:** "How does it feel when..."
5. **Compounding:** "This problem only gets worse over time"

**Warning:** Don't overdo it. Agitate enough to motivate, not so much you depress or alienate.

---

### S - Solution

**Goal:** Present your product/service as the answer to their agitated problem.

**Techniques:**
- Bridge directly from the problem to your solution
- Explain how it works (your unique mechanism)
- Focus on transformation, not just features
- Include proof (testimonials, results, data)
- Make the path to relief clear

**Good Examples:**
- "That's why we built [Product]. It automates data entry so your team can focus on selling."
- "[Product] uses AI to identify exactly what content will rank—so you stop guessing and start growing."
- "With our proven framework, you'll turn first calls into closed deals. Here's how it works..."

**Solution Structure:**
1. Introduce the solution
2. Explain the mechanism (how/why it works)
3. Show proof (testimonials, case studies)
4. Detail the benefits
5. Call to action

## PAS in Action: Full Example

**Problem:**
> "You're creating content every week, but your organic traffic is stuck. Despite hours of writing, researching, and publishing, the needle isn't moving."

**Agitate:**
> "Meanwhile, you're watching competitors outrank you for keywords you should own. Every month that passes is another month of leads going to them instead of you. And let's be honest—leadership is starting to notice. 'Why isn't content working?' is a question you dread hearing."

**Solution:**
> "ContentIQ changes everything. Our AI analyzes the top-ranking pages for any keyword and tells you exactly what to include—topics, questions, word count, even semantic terms. No more guessing. Just data-driven content that ranks. In fact, users see an average 47% increase in organic traffic within 90 days."

## PAS Website Section Mapping

| Website Section | PAS Element | Purpose |
|----------------|-------------|---------|
| Hero headline | Problem | Name it immediately |
| Subheadline | Problem/Agitate | Expand and intensify |
| Problem section | Agitate | Twist the knife |
| Solution section | Solution | Introduce your answer |
| How it works | Solution | Explain mechanism |
| Testimonials | Solution | Prove it works |
| CTA | Solution | Path to relief |

## PAS vs. Other Frameworks

| Framework | Best For |
|-----------|----------|
| PAS | Pain-point driven products, B2B, services |
| AIDA | Awareness building, aspirational products |
| BAB (Before-After-Bridge) | Transformation stories |
| FAB (Features-Advantages-Benefits) | Technical products |

## PAS Variations

### PASO (Problem-Agitate-Solution-Outcome)
Adds an Outcome section showing the transformation after using the solution.

### PASOS (Problem-Agitate-Solution-Outcome-Sale)
Adds an explicit sales/CTA element at the end.

### PASTOR
Problem, Amplify, Story, Transformation, Offer, Response

## Common PAS Mistakes

1. **Weak problem:** Being too vague or generic
2. **No agitation:** Jumping straight to solution
3. **Over-agitation:** Making readers feel hopeless
4. **Solution disconnection:** Solution doesn't clearly address the problem
5. **Features over outcomes:** Focusing on what it does, not what it solves

## When to Use PAS

**PAS works best for:**
- Problem-aware audiences (see `buyer-awareness-levels.md`)
- Pain-point driven products
- B2B and professional services
- Products that save time/money
- Products that fix something broken

**By Awareness Level (see `buyer-awareness-levels.md`):**
| Awareness Level | PAS Application |
|-----------------|-----------------|
| Unaware | Not ideal — they don't recognize the problem yet |
| Problem-Aware | **Perfect fit** — agitate their known pain |
| Solution-Aware | Problem briefly, focus on Solution differentiation |
| Product-Aware | Light Problem reminder, heavy Solution proof |
| Most Aware | Skip to Solution/offer |

**Consider alternatives for:**
- Aspiration-driven products: Use AIDA (see `aida-framework.md`)
- Unaware audiences: Use content marketing first, then AIDA
- Commoditized products: Differentiate with unique mechanism

**Combining PAS + Cialdini (see `cialdini-principles.md`):**
| PAS Stage | Best Cialdini Principles |
|-----------|-------------------------|
| Problem | Liking (empathy, "we understand") |
| Agitate | Scarcity (of time/opportunity), Authority (data) |
| Solution | Social Proof, Authority, Reciprocity |

## PAS Headlines

**Problem Headlines:**
- "Still struggling with [problem]?"
- "Tired of [negative situation]?"
- "[Problem] costing you [consequence]?"

**Agitate Headlines:**
- "The real cost of [problem] (it's worse than you think)"
- "Why [problem] is only getting worse"
- "How [problem] is silently killing your [metric]"

**Solution Headlines:**
- "Finally, a way to [solve problem]"
- "How [customers] eliminated [problem] in [timeframe]"
- "The [adjective] solution to [problem]"

# Cialdini's 7 Principles of Persuasion

**Source:** Dr. Robert Cialdini, "Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion" (1984), updated with 7th principle in "Pre-Suasion" (2016)

These principles explain why people say "yes" and how to apply them ethically in website copy.

**Related Frameworks:**
- Use with AIDA (see `aida-framework.md`) — Cialdini principles enhance each AIDA stage
- Use with PAS (see `pas-framework.md`) — Apply principles in the Solution phase
- Match to awareness levels (see `buyer-awareness-levels.md`) — Different principles work at different stages

## The 7 Principles

### 1. Reciprocity

**Principle:** When someone gives us something, we feel compelled to give something back.

**Website Applications:**
- Free trials, freemium tiers
- Valuable free content (guides, tools, templates)
- Free consultations or assessments
- Lead magnets that provide genuine value

**Copy Examples:**
- "Download our free [valuable resource]"
- "Get instant access to [tool] - no credit card required"
- "Here's a free template to help you [solve problem]"

**Best Practice:** Give value first, before asking for anything in return.

---

### 2. Commitment & Consistency

**Principle:** Once people commit to something (even small), they're more likely to follow through with larger commitments to stay consistent.

**Website Applications:**
- Micro-commitments before big asks (quiz → email → purchase)
- Multi-step forms (start easy, escalate)
- Free trials that require setup investment
- "Save for later" or wishlist features

**Copy Examples:**
- "Start with a simple question: What's your biggest challenge?"
- "You're already halfway there - complete your profile"
- "Join 10,000+ marketers who've made the switch"

**Best Practice:** Start with small, easy yeses before the big ask.

---

### 3. Social Proof

**Principle:** People look to others' actions to determine their own, especially when uncertain.

**Website Applications:**
- Customer testimonials with photos and names
- Client logos (especially recognizable brands)
- User counts ("Join 50,000+ customers")
- Star ratings and reviews
- Case studies with specific results
- "Most popular" badges
- Real-time activity ("Sarah just purchased...")

**Copy Examples:**
- "Trusted by teams at Google, Airbnb, and Stripe"
- "4.9/5 stars from 2,000+ reviews"
- "See why 50,000 marketers choose [product]"

**Best Practice:** Use specific numbers and recognizable names. Vague social proof ("many customers") is less effective.

---

### 4. Authority

**Principle:** People follow the lead of credible, knowledgeable experts.

**Website Applications:**
- Expert endorsements
- Certifications, awards, credentials
- Media mentions ("As seen in...")
- Industry association memberships
- Author credentials and bios
- Data and research citations

**Copy Examples:**
- "Recommended by [industry expert]"
- "Winner of [prestigious award]"
- "As featured in Forbes, TechCrunch, and Inc."
- "Based on research from [university/institution]"

**Best Practice:** Display authority signals near key decision points (pricing, CTAs).

---

### 5. Liking

**Principle:** People prefer to say yes to those they like. Liking increases through similarity, compliments, and cooperation.

**Website Applications:**
- Relatable "About Us" pages showing real people
- Brand personality that mirrors target audience
- Storytelling that creates connection
- Showing you understand their challenges
- Friendly, conversational tone

**Copy Examples:**
- "We started [company] because we faced the same problem you're facing"
- "Built by marketers, for marketers"
- "We get it - [acknowledge their frustration]"

**Best Practice:** Show similarity to your audience. Use "we" language that includes them.

---

### 6. Scarcity

**Principle:** People want more of what there's less of. Scarcity creates urgency.

**Website Applications:**
- Limited-time offers with deadlines
- Limited quantity ("Only 3 spots left")
- Exclusive access
- Early-bird pricing
- Seasonal availability

**Copy Examples:**
- "Offer ends [specific date]"
- "Only 5 seats remaining at this price"
- "Exclusive access for founding members"
- "This bonus disappears in [countdown]"

**Best Practice:** Scarcity must be genuine. Fake urgency destroys trust. Use sparingly.

---

### 7. Unity

**Principle:** People are influenced by those they perceive as "one of us" - shared identity creates powerful bonds.

**Website Applications:**
- Community membership emphasis
- Shared values and mission
- "People like us do things like this"
- Insider language and terminology
- Exclusive groups or communities

**Copy Examples:**
- "Join the [tribe/community/movement]"
- "For founders who refuse to settle"
- "You're not just buying a product - you're joining a movement"
- "Made for [specific identity group]"

**Best Practice:** Identify what tribe your audience belongs to and speak to that identity.

---

## How to Apply on Websites

| Section | Best Principles to Use |
|---------|----------------------|
| Hero | Social Proof, Authority |
| Problem | Liking (empathy) |
| Solution | Reciprocity (value preview) |
| Features | Authority (expertise) |
| Testimonials | Social Proof, Unity |
| Pricing | Scarcity, Commitment |
| CTA | Reciprocity, Commitment |

## Ethical Guidelines

- **Never fabricate** social proof, testimonials, or scarcity
- **Be genuine** - these principles work because they reflect real psychology
- **Provide real value** - reciprocity only works with genuine generosity
- **Respect autonomy** - persuade, don't manipulate

## When to Use Cialdini Principles

**Best For:**
- Any conversion-focused page
- Landing pages and sales pages
- Email marketing
- Product pages
- Pricing pages

**By Awareness Level (see `buyer-awareness-levels.md`):**
| Awareness Level | Most Effective Principles |
|-----------------|--------------------------|
| Unaware | Liking (storytelling), Unity (shared identity) |
| Problem-Aware | Liking (empathy), Authority (expertise) |
| Solution-Aware | Social Proof, Authority |
| Product-Aware | Social Proof, Scarcity, Commitment |
| Most Aware | Scarcity, Reciprocity (bonuses) |

**Combined with Other Frameworks:**
- **AIDA + Cialdini:** Use Authority in Attention, Social Proof in Desire, Scarcity in Action
- **PAS + Cialdini:** Use Liking in Problem (empathy), Authority/Social Proof in Solution

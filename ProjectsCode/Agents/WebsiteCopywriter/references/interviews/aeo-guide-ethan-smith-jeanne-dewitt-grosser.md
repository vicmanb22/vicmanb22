# Part One: Understanding AEO

## What Is Answer Engine Optimization?

Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) is the practice of optimizing your content and web presence to appear in the answers generated by AI-powered systems like ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Perplexity.

As Ethan Smith explained:

> "AEO and GEO are essentially trying to describe the same thing, which is: How do I show up in LLMs as an answer? I personally prefer Answer Engine Optimization versus Generative Engine Optimization because 'generative' can mean generating images and videos and things other than an answer, whereas 'answer' is more narrowly defined."

## Why AEO Matters Now

The shift toward AI-powered search is accelerating. Companies are seeing meaningful traffic and conversions from LLM referrals—and the quality of those leads is exceptional.

Ethan shared concrete data:

> "Webflow saw a 6x conversion rate difference between LLM traffic and Google search traffic... significantly more qualified. I think that's probably because you're so primed because you're having a conversation with multiple follow-ups, and so there's so much intent that you've built."

Webflow now gets **8% of their signups from LLMs**, making it one of their top acquisition channels.

Jeanne DeWitt Grosser at Vercel echoed this priority:

> "Performance drives SEO. SEO is an input into AEO. And everybody's thinking about AEO right now."

## How LLMs Generate Answers

Understanding the underlying mechanics is essential for optimization. When ChatGPT or similar tools answer a question, they use a two-step process:

**Step 1: Retrieval (RAG)** The LLM runs a search to find relevant sources—this is called Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG). It identifies citations that might contain useful information.

**Step 2: Generation** The LLM synthesizes information from those citations into a coherent answer, deciding what to include, how to weight different sources, and how to present the information.

Ethan clarified the distinction:

> "There's the core model and then there's RAG. The core model is: I'm looking at Common Crawl and billions of web pages and then I'm retraining the model... Then there's RAG, and RAG basically means Retrieval-Augmented Generation. So I'm going to do a search and then I'm going to summarize the search. These are two different things. Most of what I'm describing is about the RAG piece, not the core model piece."

This means AEO optimization is primarily about **getting retrieved as a citation** and **containing content worth extracting into the answer**—not about influencing the base training data.

# Part Two: How AEO Differs from SEO

## The Core Difference: Summarization vs. Ranking

In traditional SEO, ranking #1 means you "win"—you get the top position and the lion's share of clicks. AEO works fundamentally differently.

Ethan explained this critical distinction:

> "Even if Webflow's URL shows up number one in the citations, they're not going to win the answer because their URL showed up number one. But in Google, they would win if their blue link showed up first. That's not the case in the LLM because the LLM is summarizing many citations. So you need to get mentioned as many times as possible."

**Key implication:** Being cited once isn't enough. You need to appear across multiple sources that the LLM retrieves. The product mentioned most frequently across citations typically gets featured most prominently in the answer.

## The Long Tail Is Much Larger

People interact with LLMs conversationally, asking follow-up questions and getting increasingly specific. This creates a vastly larger "long tail" of queries.

Ethan shared the data:

> "The average number of words—I think Perplexity said this—was around 25 words, versus Google where it's around six words. So the tail is just much, much larger. People are asking lots of follow-up questions... there's probably questions that have never been asked before, questions that have never been searched for before, because search can't support lots of really specific stuff, whereas chat is specifically made to ask a bunch of follow-up questions."

**Key implication:** You can win queries that never existed before. Hyper-specific questions about your product, use cases, and integrations are now searchable in ways they never were with traditional search.

## Early-Stage Companies Can Win

Traditional SEO favors established players with high domain authority. AEO opens opportunities for newcomers.

Ethan explained why this matters for startups:

> "When startups come to me and ask me for SEO help, my first response is: don't do it at all. Spend your time on something else because you're not going to be able to grow SEO early on in search because you don't have enough domain authority... That's not the case for Answer Engine Optimization because you can get mentioned by a citation tomorrow and start showing up immediately. You can have a Reddit thread, you can have a YouTube video, you can be mentioned on a blog. A brand new Y Combinator company launches, everyone's talking about them—they could show up in an answer tomorrow."

**Key implication:** If you're early-stage, prioritize AEO and citation-building over traditional SEO. You can compete immediately.

## Citation Overlap Is Lower Than Expected

Don't assume your Google rankings translate to LLM citations. Ethan's research found surprising divergence:

> "ChatGPT citations and Google search results are actually not that similar. Perplexity is actually interestingly more similar to Google than ChatGPT. We did a study looking at thousands of questions and saw the citation overlap with Google search results was around 35% for ChatGPT and Google."

**Key implication:** Optimize specifically for AEO—don't rely on your SEO success to carry over.

## What Stays the Same

Despite these differences, the fundamentals of quality content still apply. As Ethan noted:

> "I would say everything that works in SEO works in AEO, but there are additional things beyond SEO that also work in AEO."

Good content, clear structure, genuine expertise, and helpful information remain essential.

# Part Three: On-Site Optimization Core Principles

These principles apply across all page types on your site.

## 1. Think in Topics, Not Keywords

Each page should target a **cluster of related questions**, not a single keyword. Map out the full range of queries someone might ask within a topic.

**For a topic like "project management software":**

- What's the best project management software?
- What's the best project management tool for small teams?
- What project management software integrates with Slack?
- Does [product] have Gantt charts?
- How much does [product] cost?
- Can [product] handle agile sprints?
- What's the difference between [product] and [competitor]?

Ethan recommended a practical approach:

> "Take your competitor's paid search data or your own, put it in chat, get questions."

Literally give your keywords to ChatGPT and ask it to generate related questions—this works well for building out your topic clusters.

## 2. Answer Every Follow-Up Question

The more questions your page answers, the more likely it gets cited across different queries. Ethan was explicit:

> "The more you answer all the questions, the better. If you don't answer a question, then you're probably not going to show up. And if you answer a question as a follow-up question and subtopic that somebody else is not answering, you're going to be more likely to show up."

**Comprehensiveness beats brevity.** Think of each page as a resource that could sustain a 20-minute conversation on its topic.

## 3. Prioritize Information Gain

LLMs are trying to surface useful, differentiated content—not regurgitated summaries. Ethan identified this as perhaps the most important factor:

> "One concept would be information gain—did you say something that somebody else didn't say? Two is: how typical are you? Are you so typical that I think you're a rewritten version of somebody else's content?"

**What creates information gain:**

- Original research and proprietary data
- Expert insights from practitioners
- Unique frameworks and ways of thinking
- Specific case studies with real numbers
- Counter-conventional perspectives backed by evidence
- Synthesis that connects ideas in new ways

**What destroys information gain:**

- Summarizing competitor content
- Generic advice without specific examples
- AI-generated content without human insight
- Matching the "typical" structure of existing articles

## 4. Lead with Direct Answers

LLMs need to extract clear information. Don't bury your insights.

**Weak opening:**

> "In today's rapidly evolving business landscape, many teams are wondering about best practices. Let me share some context before diving into the answer..."

**Strong opening:**

> "The most effective approach to [topic] involves three core principles: [X], [Y], and [Z]. Here's why each matters and how to implement them..."

Get to the value immediately, then elaborate.

## 5. Structure for Extraction

Make your content easy for LLMs to parse:

- **Descriptive headers** that match question phrasing
- **Clear definitions** when introducing concepts
- **Tables and structured data** for comparisons
- **TL;DR summaries** at the beginning
- **Key takeaway callouts** throughout
- **Logical organization** with standalone sections

## 6. Demonstrate Expertise

Content attributed to credible experts performs better. Include:

- **Author credentials** and relevant experience
- **Methodology disclosure** (how do you know this?)
- **Expert quotes** with attribution
- **Real examples** from your actual experience

Ethan noted:

> "Ideally, did you do original research and do you have some domain expertise and did you mention that in the content? This is a great heuristic for just content in general."

# Part Four: Commercial Pages

Commercial pages serve **transactional and consideration intent**—someone evaluating whether your product solves their problem. These include product pages, persona pages, use case pages, comparison pages, and landing pages.

## Product Pages

### Purpose

Answer: "What is [product]?" "What does it do?" "Does it have [feature]?" "How much does it cost?"

### Key Optimization Strategies

**1. Lead with Clear, Extractable Positioning**

Your opening content should immediately communicate:

- What is this product? (category)
- Who is it for? (target user)
- What core problem does it solve?
- What makes it different?

**Bad example:**

> "Transform your workflow with the power of innovation. Join thousands of forward-thinking teams."

**Good example:**

> "Acme is a project management platform for software development teams with 10-500 employees. It combines sprint planning, bug tracking, and documentation in one tool, with native integrations for GitHub, GitLab, and Bitbucket."

**2. Comprehensive Feature Documentation**

Cover the full range of "can it do X?" queries:

- Feature name and one-line description
- How it works (brief explanation)
- Who benefits (which users/roles)
- Technical details (limits, requirements)
- What's NOT included (be explicit about limitations)

Being explicit about limitations builds trust and helps LLMs give accurate answers.

**3. Complete Integration Information**

Integration questions are extremely common: "Does [product] integrate with [tool]?" or "What [category] tools work with [tool I already use]?"

Document:

- Every integration with brief descriptions
- Categories: Native, API, third-party connectors (Zapier, etc.)
- Technical details: API availability, webhooks, sync frequency
- Workarounds when no native integration exists

Ethan highlighted an opportunity here:

> "I said, 'Which meeting transcription tool integrates with Looker?' And the answer is: none of them... There wasn't a help center article about that because it's a very obscure use case, but it's not a zero use case."

**4. Transparent Pricing**

If pricing is public, make it comprehensive:

- All plan tiers with inclusions
- Per-seat vs. flat pricing clarity
- Usage limits at each tier
- Overage costs
- Annual vs. monthly pricing
- Free tier details and limitations
- Enterprise pricing model (even if "contact us")

Hidden pricing means lost citations when someone asks "What does [product] cost?"

**5. Technical Specifications**

Document:

- Supported platforms/browsers/OS
- System requirements
- Security certifications (SOC 2, GDPR, HIPAA)
- Data residency options
- Uptime SLAs
- API rate limits

### Product Page Structure Template

```
[H1: Product Name - Brief Category Description]

[Opening: What it is, who it's for, core value prop]

## What [Product] Does
[Comprehensive feature breakdown by capability area]

## Who Uses [Product]
[User types with specific use cases]

## Integrations
[Complete list organized by category]

## Pricing
[Transparent tier breakdown]

## Technical Specifications
[Requirements, compliance, security]

## How [Product] Compares
[Honest positioning vs. alternatives]

## Getting Started
[Clear next steps, implementation overview]
```

## Persona Pages

### Purpose

Answer: "What's the best [category] for [role/team type]?"

### Key Optimization Strategies

**1. Be Specific About the Persona**

Don't create generic pages. Get specific:

- Not: "For Marketing Teams"
- Better: "For B2B SaaS Marketing Teams Under 50 Employees"

Specificity matches specific LLM queries and reduces competition.

**2. Lead with Persona-Specific Pain Points**

Articulate the challenges this persona faces. This helps LLMs match your page to problem-focused queries.

**3. Map Features to Persona-Specific Benefits**

Same feature, different framing:

- **For developers:** "Git-based version control lets you manage infrastructure through your existing PR workflow"
- **For compliance teams:** "Git-based version control creates an audit trail of every change"

**4. Include Persona-Specific Proof**

Reference customers and case studies from this specific persona type.

**5. Address Persona-Specific Objections**

Each persona has different concerns:

- **Developers:** flexibility, learning curve, speed impact
- **Finance:** ROI, total cost, vendor stability
- **Security:** compliance, access controls, data handling

### Persona Page Structure Template

```
[H1: Product Name for [Specific Persona]]

[Opening: Acknowledge specific challenges]

## Why [Persona] Choose [Product]
[Top 3-5 reasons mapped to their priorities]

## Key Capabilities for [Persona]
[Features as persona-specific benefits]

## How [Persona Type] Use [Product]
[Specific workflows and use cases]

## [Persona Type] Customer Stories
[Relevant case studies]

## Common Questions from [Persona Type]
[FAQ addressing persona-specific concerns]

## Getting Started
[Persona-appropriate onboarding path]
```

## Use Case Pages

### Purpose

Answer: "How do I [accomplish specific goal]?" "What's the best tool for [specific task]?"

### Key Optimization Strategies

**1. Define the Use Case Precisely**

- Not: "Analytics"
- Better: "Tracking Product Usage and Feature Adoption"
- Best: "Measuring Feature Adoption to Prioritize Your Roadmap"

**2. Describe the Problem Before the Solution**

Articulate:

- What is the user trying to accomplish?
- Why is this challenging?
- What do bad solutions look like?
- What does success look like?

**3. Show the Complete Workflow**

Walk through:

1. Getting started (setup, data connection)
2. Specific steps in the workflow
3. What the output looks like
4. How to act on results

**4. Cover Adjacent Use Cases**

Link to related capabilities:

- "Once you've set up X, you might also want to..."
- "Teams who use this for X often also use it for Y..."

**5. Include Quantified Outcomes**

Specific metrics beat vague claims:

- "Teams typically see 40% reduction in time spent on X"
- "Average implementation time is 2 weeks"
- "Customers report 3x improvement in Y"

### Use Case Page Structure Template

```
[H1: How to [Accomplish Specific Outcome] with [Product]]

## The Challenge
[Problem description]

## The Solution
[How your product approaches this]

## How It Works
[Step-by-step workflow]

## Results You Can Expect
[Quantified outcomes and timeline]

## Example: [Customer Story]
[Real example in action]

## Related Use Cases
[Links to adjacent capabilities]

## Get Started
[Specific CTA for this use case]
```

## Comparison and Alternative Pages

### Purpose

Answer: "[Product A] vs [Product B]?" "Alternatives to [Product]?"

### Key Optimization Strategies

**1. Be Genuinely Honest**

Paradoxically, honest comparison pages perform better. Acknowledging competitor strengths builds credibility.

**2. Structure for Direct Comparison**

Use tables for easy extraction:

|Capability|Your Product|Competitor|
|---|---|---|
|Feature A|✓ All plans|✓ Enterprise only|
|Feature B|✓ Native|Via integration|
|Pricing|$X/month|$Y/month|

**3. Acknowledge Where Competitors Excel**

- "[Competitor] is better if you need [capability] because..."
- "If [requirement] is your priority, [Competitor] might fit better"

**4. Cover the Full Competitive Landscape**

Create pages for:

- Head-to-head comparisons (You vs. Competitor A)
- Category alternatives (Alternatives to [Your Product])
- Migration guides (Switching from [Competitor] to [You])

**5. Update Regularly**

Add "last updated" dates and review quarterly. Outdated comparisons lose credibility.

# Part Five: Content Pages

Content pages serve **informational and educational intent**—someone learning about a topic or researching a problem. These include blogs, articles, comprehensive guides, podcasts, and tutorials.

## Blog Posts and Articles

### Purpose

Answer: "How do I...?" "What are best practices for...?" "What should I consider when...?"

### Key Optimization Strategies

**1. Information Gain Is Everything**

Ethan was emphatic:

> "One concept would be information gain—did you say something that somebody else didn't say? Two is: how typical are you? Are you so typical that I think you're a rewritten version of somebody else's content?"

**Sources of information gain:**

- Original research and surveys
- Proprietary data from your user base
- Expert interviews with practitioners
- Unique frameworks you've developed
- Counter-conventional takes with evidence
- Detailed case studies with real numbers
- Synthesis connecting previously unconnected ideas

**2. Match Winning Content Formats**

Research what's getting cited for your target topics:

- **Definitive guides:** Comprehensive topic coverage
- **Listicles:** "X Best Tools for Y"
- **How-to tutorials:** Step-by-step with specifics
- **Comparison posts:** "X vs Y: Which Is Right?"
- **Data-driven posts:** Original research with analysis
- **Expert roundups:** Curated practitioner perspectives

**3. Answer the Full Topic**

Structure content to anticipate follow-up questions. If writing about "how to run effective 1:1 meetings," also cover:

- How often should you have them?
- How long should they be?
- What questions should you ask?
- How do you handle difficult conversations?
- What should you document?
- How does this change for remote teams?

**4. Include Expert Attribution**

- Author bio with credentials
- Expert quotes with attribution
- Methodology disclosure

**5. Maintain and Update**

- Visible "last updated" dates
- Quarterly reviews of high-value content
- Add new developments as they emerge

## Comprehensive Guides

### Purpose

Answer: "Tell me everything about [topic]"

These are your pillar content—definitive resources that can answer dozens of related questions.

### Key Optimization Strategies

**1. Truly Comprehensive Coverage**

Map every question someone might ask. Mine sources:

- "People Also Ask" from Google
- ChatGPT-generated related questions
- Community forums (Reddit, Stack Overflow)
- Customer support tickets
- Sales team common questions

**2. Logical, Hierarchical Structure**

- Chapter/section structure for long guides
- Progressive complexity (basics → advanced)
- Clear navigation (table of contents, anchor links)
- Standalone sections that make sense independently

**3. Include Original Frameworks**

Develop and name your own ways of thinking. Named frameworks get cited and remembered—like Shreyas Doshi's LNO Framework or Gibson Biddle's DHM Model.

**4. Mix Media Types**

- Text explanations
- Visual diagrams
- Data and charts
- Examples and screenshots
- Downloadable templates

**5. Treat as Living Documents**

- Add sections as topics evolve
- Update examples with fresh data
- Incorporate reader questions
- Version and date-stamp updates

# Part Six: Help Center Optimization

This was one of Ethan's most tactical recommendations. Help centers are goldmines for AEO because they naturally answer the specific, long-tail questions people ask in chat.

## Why Help Centers Matter for AEO

Help center content answers the exact questions buyers ask:

- "Does [product] support [feature]?"
- "How do I [accomplish task] in [product]?"
- "Can [product] integrate with [tool]?"

These hyper-specific queries are exactly what the expanded long tail of LLM conversations captures.

## Key Optimization Strategies

### 1. Move to a Subdirectory

Ethan was explicit:

> "It's frequently on a subdomain. For whatever reason, subdomains don't work as well as subdirectories. So move it to a subdirectory."

- **Bad:** help.yoursite.com
- **Good:** yoursite.com/help

### 2. Cross-Link Aggressively

Help center pages rarely link to each other. Create logical pathways between related articles.

Ethan noted:

> "Usually you do not have optimized internal links. So link from help center page to help center page. Make sure there's good cross-linking."

### 3. Cover the Long Tail

Your help center probably covers "head" queries (basic how-tos) but misses specific edge cases.

**Mine questions from:**

- Sales call transcripts
- Customer support tickets
- Community forums and Reddit
- Social media questions
- Chatbot conversation logs
- G2/Capterra reviews

Ethan recommended:

> "What are all the questions people are asking you in your sales calls, customer support, on Reddit? Mine all those questions that exist somewhere else. Probably those same questions are being asked in chat."

### 4. Open to Community

Consider letting users contribute:

> "I might even open up to the community. Anyone can ask anything, because the community will then fill in the tail and answer those. And again, in many cases, there might be nobody talking about this at all. So you could be the only citation for this and then win that tail of questions."

If you're the **only source** answering a specific question, you win by default.

# Part Seven: Off-Site Optimization

On-site optimization alone isn't enough. Because LLMs summarize across multiple citations, you need to appear across the sources they retrieve.

## Understanding Citations

Ethan broke down the citation landscape:

> "Citations are comprised of video—YouTube, Vimeo—UGC like Reddit and Quora, affiliates, Dotdash Meredith which is showing up all over the place, and blogs."

Different strategies are needed for each citation type.

## YouTube and Video

### Why It Matters

Video content is heavily cited but underutilized for B2B topics.

Ethan explained the opportunity:

> "The interesting thing with this, especially for B2B, is that YouTube, Vimeo, other video sites—the kinds of things people make videos for are food, traveling, fun, beauty. There's not that many videos about AI-powered payment processing APIs, as interesting as that is, but it's a great money term. So if you make a video for these really specific, high-LTV, maybe non-glamorous keywords, questions, topics—that's actually a big opportunity."

### Strategy

- Create videos for specific, valuable questions in your space
- Optimize titles and descriptions with target questions
- Include comprehensive descriptions that LLMs can parse
- No community gatekeeping—you control the content

## Reddit

### Why It Matters

Reddit is heavily cited in LLM answers because it represents authentic human opinions managed by community moderation.

### The Wrong Approach

Ethan warned against spam tactics:

> "The obvious strategy for a growth person is: let's make a bunch of automated spam and run it all over the place... What are people looking at? They're looking at creating hundreds of fake Reddit accounts, pretending to be someone that you're not... Fortunately, that doesn't work very well... We're seeing people trying to do that, and we're also seeing those accounts get banned and those comments get deleted."

### The Right Approach

Be a genuine, transparent participant:

> "The strategy is: find a thread that is part of a citation that you want to show up in, say who you are, say where you work, and then give a useful piece of information. And that works really well. That sounds simple if you're not in the growth mindset of 'I need to scale this to hundreds of comments,' but you don't actually need 10,000 comments. Even five could be great."

**Practical approach:**

1. Create a real Reddit account
2. Identify threads relevant to your product's topics
3. Contribute genuinely useful information
4. Be transparent: "I work at [Company], and..."
5. Don't oversell—provide value

## Affiliates and Publications

### Tier-One Affiliates

Publications like Forbes Advisor, Dotdash Meredith properties (Good Housekeeping, etc.), and major review sites are heavily cited.

Ethan noted:

> "Paying an affiliate to mention you—that's pretty easy if you have the money. If you want to be the best credit card, you pay Forbes and then you're the best credit card."

### Strategy by Tier

**Tier-one affiliates:** Expensive but controllable. Major publications with high citation rates.

**Tier-two affiliates:** More affordable, still valuable. Industry-specific publications and review sites.

**Blogs and industry sites:** Build relationships for mentions and coverage.

## Other UGC Platforms

- **Quora:** Similar to Reddit—genuine, helpful answers with transparency
- **Industry forums:** Niche communities relevant to your space
- **Product review sites:** G2, Capterra, TrustRadius for B2B

## The Citation Optimization Process

1. **Identify target questions** you want to win
2. **Analyze current citations** for those questions
3. **Map citation types** (video, UGC, affiliates, blogs)
4. **Develop strategy for each type**
5. **Execute and track impact**

# Part Eight: Technical Considerations

## Schema Markup

Implement relevant schema to help LLMs understand your content:

|Page Type|Recommended Schema|
|---|---|
|Product pages|Product, Offer, Review, AggregateRating|
|How-to content|HowTo, Step|
|FAQ sections|FAQPage, Question, Answer|
|Articles|Article, Author, Organization|
|Comparison pages|Product (multiple), ItemList|
|Podcast episodes|PodcastEpisode, AudioObject|

Schema is especially important for commerce queries, where Ethan noted:

> "If you ask 'what's the best TV for apartments,' there are actual shoppable cards. Those shoppable cards are showing multiple sellers. Those sellers have rich snippets—schema is important, the number of reviews are important."

## Indexing Configuration

Allow LLM indexing while optionally blocking training.

Ethan explained the distinction:

> "You can index my site but please don't train on my data. And they have different user agents for that and different bots. So you can just put in your robots.txt: 'This training bot—not allowed. Index bot—you are allowed.'"

**Sample robots.txt:**

```
# Allow LLM indexing
User-agent: GPTBot
Allow: /

User-agent: ChatGPT-User
Allow: /

User-agent: anthropic-ai
Allow: /

User-agent: PerplexityBot
Allow: /

# Block training if desired (check current bot names)
User-agent: GPTBot
Disallow: /private-content/
```

## Why You Must Allow Indexing

Ethan echoed advice from Brian Balfour:

> "It's not your choice whether to play the game. You are playing the game whether you want to or not. So you might as well try to show up. If you just say 'don't look at any of my data,' then you cannot show up and your competitors will."

Blocking indexing means losing to competitors who don't.

## Page Speed and Technical Health

While less directly measurable for AEO than SEO, fundamentals still matter:

- Fast page load times
- Mobile-friendly design
- Clean, crawlable structure
- No broken links or errors

# Part Nine: Measurement and Experimentation

## Tracking Share of Voice

Traditional keyword tracking doesn't translate to AEO because:

- **Answer variance:** Same question produces different answers each time
- **Question variance:** Different phrasings yield different results
- **Multiple surfaces:** ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude all differ

Ethan recommended tracking **share of voice**:

> "You essentially need to create a share of voice across all these different things—a distribution. How often am I showing up? What's my average rank?"

### What to Track

- **Frequency:** How often you appear in answers for target questions
- **Position:** Where you appear in the answer (first mentioned, cited, etc.)
- **Citation rate:** How often your URLs appear as sources
- **Surface coverage:** Performance across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude

### Tools

There are now ~60 AEO tracking tools. Ethan's advice:

> "Pick the cheapest one that does what you need, just like keyword tracking. There's not a premium version of keyword tracking. You rank number three or you don't."

## Running Experiments

This is critical. Ethan was emphatic that most "best practices" aren't validated:

> "The majority of the information and best practices are not correct. And the reason why is because people don't do analysis. Somebody will say something and then it will get repeated and then it becomes best practice, and no one ever did analysis."

### Experiment Design

**1. Select target questions** Choose 100-200 target questions for your experiment.

**2. Split into test and control**

- Control group: 50% of questions you won't touch
- Test group: 50% you'll intervene on

**3. Baseline tracking** Track share of voice for 2-3 weeks before making changes.

**4. Intervene on test group only** Make your changes (new content, Reddit comments, YouTube videos, etc.) only for test group questions.

**5. Measure post-intervention** Track for 2-3 weeks after changes.

**6. Compare results** If test group improved and control didn't, your intervention worked.

**7. Reproduce** Ethan emphasized reproducibility:

> "Reproducibility is very important. Try to do that study multiple times... If it works 10 times, then it probably works."

### What to Test

- Content format changes
- Help center additions
- Reddit commenting strategy
- YouTube video creation
- Schema markup implementation
- Internal linking improvements

## Measuring Conversions

AEO attribution is challenging because users don't always click through directly.

Ethan explained the issue:

> "What they will do is: they will open a new tab and they will type in the brand name and they will go to Google and then they'll click on your domain. And you will think that it was branded Google search when it wasn't. Or they'll open up a new tab and they will type in your domain and they'll go directly to your domain, and you will falsely think that it was direct traffic."

### Attribution Approaches

**1. Direct tracking** Track referral traffic from LLM domains (chat.openai.com, perplexity.ai, etc.)

**2. Post-conversion surveys** Ask "How did you hear about us?" to capture LLM-influenced conversions.

**3. Share of voice correlation** Track share of voice improvements alongside conversion changes.

**4. Branded search analysis** Monitor branded search volume—increases may indicate LLM-driven awareness.

# Part Eleven: Common Mistakes to Avoid

## 1. Relying on AI-Generated Content

Ethan's research was definitive:

> "Around 10 to 12% of content in Google search and in ChatGPT are AI-generated. 90% are not."

Purely AI-generated content (without human editing and original insight) doesn't work and will likely be increasingly penalized.

**AI-assisted is fine.** Human-edited with original insight added is the path forward. Pure automation is not.

## 2. Assuming Google Success = AEO Success

With only 35% citation overlap between Google and ChatGPT, your SEO rankings don't guarantee AEO performance. Optimize specifically for answer engines.

## 3. Ignoring the Long Tail

The biggest AEO opportunity is answering questions no one else answers. Hyper-specific queries may have low individual volume but:

- You can win them easily (no competition)
- Collectively they add up significantly
- Leads are extremely high-intent

## 4. Neglecting Help Centers

Help centers are underoptimized goldmines. They naturally answer the specific questions people ask in LLM conversations.

## 5. Spamming Reddit

Inauthentic Reddit activity gets detected and banned. Genuine, transparent participation works; spam doesn't.

## 6. Not Running Experiments

Most AEO "best practices" aren't validated. Test interventions with proper control groups before scaling.

## 7. Hiding Pricing and Specifications

Every piece of information you hide is a question you can't be cited for. Transparency creates citation opportunities.

## 8. Creating Thin Landing Pages

Landing pages optimized purely for conversion (hero, bullets, CTA) won't perform for AEO. You need substantive content that can be cited.

## 9. Forgetting to Update Content

Stale content loses credibility. Add update dates and review high-value content quarterly.

## 10. Treating AEO as Separate from SEO

While there are important differences, the fundamentals overlap significantly. Integrate AEO into your existing content strategy rather than treating it as entirely separate.

## Summary: The AEO Mindset

AEO success comes down to a few core principles:

**1. Be the most comprehensive answer.** LLMs summarize information. The more questions your content answers, the more likely you get cited.

**2. Add genuine information gain.** Original research, unique frameworks, expert insights, and specific examples differentiate you from regurgitated content.

**3. Appear across multiple citations.** On-site optimization isn't enough. Build presence across the sources LLMs retrieve—YouTube, Reddit, affiliates, publications.

**4. Measure and experiment.** Most "best practices" aren't validated. Run controlled experiments and reproduce results before scaling.

**5. Optimize for the long tail.** The expanded query length in LLM conversations creates opportunities to win hyper-specific questions no one else is answering.

The companies winning at AEO are treating it as both an evolution of SEO and a distinct channel requiring specific optimization. The fundamentals of quality content remain, but the strategies for getting that content surfaced in AI-generated answers require new thinking.

As Ethan noted, the channel is growing rapidly and the leads are significantly more qualified. The time to invest is now.

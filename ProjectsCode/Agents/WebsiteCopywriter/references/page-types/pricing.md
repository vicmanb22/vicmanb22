# Pricing Page Copy Guide

The pricing page is where consideration turns to decision. Clear, confident pricing communication directly impacts conversion rates.

---

## Pricing Page Purpose

**Primary goals:**
1. Communicate value clearly before price
2. Help visitors choose the right plan
3. Reduce friction in the purchase decision
4. Address price-related objections
5. Convert browsers to buyers

**Key principle:** Price is only a concern when value is unclear.

---

## Pricing Page Structure

### Recommended Sections

1. **Header/Hero** - Positioning statement, value framing
2. **Pricing Table/Cards** - Plans with features and CTAs
3. **Feature Comparison** - Detailed breakdown (if needed)
4. **Social Proof** - Value-focused testimonials
5. **FAQ** - Price and purchase objections
6. **Final CTA** - For those still undecided
7. **Enterprise/Contact Option** - For custom needs

## Pricing Table Copy

### Plan Naming

**Naming approaches:**
| Approach | Example | Best For |
|----------|---------|----------|
| Persona-based | Starter, Professional, Enterprise | B2B SaaS |
| Size-based | Basic, Plus, Pro | Consumer products |
| Tier-based | Free, Growth, Scale | Freemium models |
| Outcome-based | Essentials, Advanced, Unlimited | Feature-driven |

**Naming tips:**
- Names should feel like upgrades, not limitations
- Avoid negative connotations ("Basic" can feel limited)
- Consider "Professional" > "Pro" for B2B

### Plan Descriptions

Each plan needs a one-line description:

**Formula:** `Best for [who] who want [what]`

**Examples:**
- Starter: "Best for individuals just getting started"
- Professional: "Best for teams who need collaboration features"
- Enterprise: "Best for organizations requiring custom solutions"

### Feature Presentation

**Per-plan feature list:**
- 5-8 most important features
- Start with what makes this plan valuable
- Use benefit language, not just feature names
- Include limits clearly (100 users, 10GB storage)

**Good feature copy:**
- ✓ "Unlimited projects"
- ✓ "Real-time collaboration for up to 10 users"
- ✓ "Priority support with 4-hour response"

**Bad feature copy:**
- ✗ "Projects" (unclear)
- ✗ "Collaboration" (what kind?)
- ✗ "Support" (how good?)

### CTA Buttons

| Plan Type | CTA Examples |
|-----------|--------------|
| Free | "Get Started Free", "Start Free" |
| Trial | "Start 14-Day Trial", "Try Free" |
| Paid | "Get Started", "Choose [Plan]", "Buy Now" |
| Enterprise | "Contact Sales", "Talk to Us", "Get Custom Quote" |

**Tips:**
- CTA should match plan intent
- Avoid "Subscribe" (feels like commitment)
- "Get Started" is universal and non-threatening

## Price Presentation

### Price Anchoring

Present prices to highlight value:

**Show savings:**
- Monthly: $29/mo
- Annual: $24/mo (save 17%)
- Annual: $24/mo ~~$29~~ (strikethrough)

**Show value:**
- "$1/day" instead of "$30/month"
- "Less than a coffee per day"
- "Pays for itself in one [outcome]"

### Price Formatting

| Format | When to Use |
|--------|-------------|
| $29/mo | Standard monthly |
| $290/yr | Annual (simple) |
| $24/mo billed annually | Annual with monthly equivalent |
| Starting at $29 | Variable pricing |
| From $29/mo | Usage-based |
| Custom | Enterprise/contact |

### Billing Clarity

Always clarify:
- Monthly vs. annual billing
- Per-user vs. flat pricing
- What's included vs. add-ons
- When billing starts (trial conversion)

## Highlighting and Recommendations

### "Most Popular" or "Recommended"

**Why it works:**
- Social proof (others chose this)
- Decision simplification
- Anchoring for comparison

**Implementation:**
- Badge or ribbon on one plan
- Slight visual emphasis (larger, highlighted border)
- Usually the middle or target plan

**Copy options:**
- "Most Popular"
- "Best Value"
- "Recommended"
- "Most Chosen by [Persona]"

### Plan Comparison Positioning

**Three-plan standard:**
1. **Low tier:** Establish floor, make middle look reasonable
2. **Middle tier:** Target plan, best value positioning
3. **High tier:** Premium option, makes middle feel accessible

## Feature Comparison Table

### When to Include

- Multiple plans with different features
- Complex products needing detailed breakdown
- Enterprise buyers who need specifics
- When plans differ beyond just limits

### Comparison Table Structure

```
| Feature            | Starter | Pro    | Enterprise |
|--------------------|---------|--------|------------|
| Users              | 1       | 10     | Unlimited  |
| Storage            | 5GB     | 50GB   | Unlimited  |
| Integrations       | 3       | All    | All        |
| API Access         | —       | ✓      | ✓          |
| Priority Support   | —       | —      | ✓          |
| Custom Training    | —       | —      | ✓          |
```

**Tips:**
- Group related features
- Checkmarks (✓) for included, dashes (—) for excluded
- Highlight differences, not similarities
- Keep most important features near top

## Social Proof on Pricing Pages

### What Works Best

Value-focused testimonials that address:
- ROI and business results
- Worth the investment
- Comparison to alternatives
- Upgrade satisfaction

**Examples:**
- "[Product] paid for itself in the first month."
- "We switched from [Competitor] and saved 40% while getting more features."
- "The Pro plan was exactly what our growing team needed."

### Placement

- Below pricing table (reinforces value)
- In FAQ section (addresses objections)
- Embedded near CTA buttons

## Pricing Page FAQ

### Questions to Address

**Value & ROI:**
- Is it worth the price?
- What do I get for my money?
- How does it compare to alternatives?

**Billing & Payment:**
- How does billing work?
- What payment methods do you accept?
- Can I change plans later?

**Trial & Risk:**
- Do you offer a free trial?
- Is there a money-back guarantee?
- Can I cancel anytime?

**Plan Selection:**
- How do I know which plan is right?
- What happens if I outgrow my plan?
- Do you offer discounts (startups, nonprofits, annual)?

### FAQ Copy Examples

**Q: Can I cancel anytime?**
A: Yes, you can cancel your subscription at any time. If you cancel, you'll continue to have access until the end of your billing period. No long-term contracts, no cancellation fees.

**Q: Which plan is right for me?**
A: Most individuals start with Starter. If you're on a team or need [key feature], Professional is the best fit. Enterprise is for organizations needing [enterprise needs]. Not sure? Start with a free trial and upgrade anytime.

**Q: Do you offer refunds?**
A: Yes, we offer a 30-day money-back guarantee. If you're not satisfied for any reason, contact us within 30 days for a full refund.

## Risk Reducers

### Money-Back Guarantee

**Placement:** Near CTAs and in FAQ
**Copy:** "30-day money-back guarantee. Try risk-free."

### Free Trial

**Clarify:**
- Trial length
- Credit card requirement (or not)
- What happens after trial
- Full feature access or limited

**Copy example:**
"14-day free trial. No credit card required. Full access to all features."

### Other Risk Reducers

- "Cancel anytime"
- "No long-term contracts"
- "Downgrade anytime"
- "Price lock guarantee"

## Enterprise/Contact Section

### When to Include

- High-value accounts need custom pricing
- Complex implementations require consultation
- Specific compliance or security needs
- Volume discounts available

### What to Include

**Enterprise plan card elements:**
- "Contact Sales" or "Talk to Us" CTA
- List of enterprise-specific features
- Note about custom pricing/solutions

**Contact form elements:**
- Company name
- Company size
- Current challenge/need
- Budget range (optional)

## Common Pricing Page Mistakes

1. **Price without context:** No value framing before showing price
2. **Too many plans:** More than 4 causes decision paralysis
3. **Unclear differences:** Hard to understand what changes between plans
4. **Hidden costs:** Surprise fees or add-ons not mentioned
5. **No recommendation:** Leaving visitors to figure out which plan
6. **Missing risk reducers:** No guarantee or trial mentioned
7. **Jargon-heavy features:** Technical terms without benefit explanation
8. **No FAQ:** Missing chance to address objections
9. **Buried enterprise option:** Hard for large buyers to find custom path
10. **Ignoring annual billing:** Missing chance to increase LTV

## Pricing Psychology

### Anchoring
- Show higher price first (or crossed out)
- Position premium plan to make mid-tier feel reasonable

### Decoy Effect
- Middle option should be obviously best value
- Low tier exists partly to make middle look good

### Loss Aversion
- Frame annual savings as "You're saving $X/year"
- Show what they miss by choosing lower tier

### Social Proof
- "Most popular" badge leverages herd behavior
- Testimonials reduce perceived risk

### Simplicity
- Three plans is ideal (two feels limited, four+ overwhelms)
- Clear differences make choice easier

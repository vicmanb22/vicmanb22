# Relational Repair Communicator

You guide relational repair before decision announcements. Good decisions land badly in fields of broken trust. Acknowledging past inconsistency creates space for new credibility.

## Core Insight

**"Decisions land in a relational field."** — Terry Real

Even a brilliant decision will be met with skepticism if trust is damaged. Repair comes BEFORE announcement, not after.

## Who Needs Repair?

"Who needs to hear about this decision?"

Categories:
- **Primary stakeholders** — Partners, cofounders, spouse
- **Team members** — Direct reports, collaborators
- **Family** — Those affected by the decision
- **Others** — Anyone whose trust matters

For each person, assess:
- What has been damaged?
- What specifically have I done?
- What acknowledgment is needed?

## The Repair-Before-Announcement Pattern

### Structure

1. **Relational Repair Opening** — Acknowledge what's happened
2. **Context (optional)** — Brief explanation without excuse
3. **Commitment** — What you're doing differently
4. **The Decision** — Only after repair
5. **Request for Support** — What you need from them

### Primary Stakeholder Script

"Let's prepare what to say to [person]. This has two parts: relational repair first, then the decision."

---

**PART 1: Relational Repair Opening**

"[Name], before I share this decision, I need to acknowledge something.

I know I've been [specific behaviors]:
- [Behavior 1]
- [Behavior 2]
- [Behavior 3]

That's affected your confidence in me, and that's fair. I'm not making excuses.

[OPTIONAL CONTEXT]
I want you to know: I've been dealing with [brief context—decide how much to share] and I'm in active recovery now. I'm back, I'm functional, and I'm committed to being reliable.

[COMMITMENT]
This decision I'm about to share—I made it using a structured process while regulated, not impulsively. I'm telling you this because I need you to trust that I'm capable of handling this 90-day experiment.

[ACCOUNTABILITY REQUEST]
If over the next 90 days you see me slipping into old patterns—[specific behaviors to watch for]—I want you to call me on it directly. Can you do that?"

---

**PART 2: Decision Announcement**

"I've completed structured analysis on [decision topic]. Based on [key factors], I've decided to [choice] for a 90-day trial.

This will [benefits].

I'm implementing starting [date]."

---

## Handling Responses

### If they look skeptical:

"I can see you have concerns. Tell me what you're thinking."

[Listen fully]

"That makes sense you'd feel that way. Fair point."

"Here's what led me to this choice: [3 reasons]. I have mitigation plans for [their concern]."

### If they say "I'm not sure this is the right call":

"I understand you might have chosen differently. I made this from a regulated state using sound process. I'm committed to 90 days and tracking [metrics]. At 90 days, we'll review together.

I'm not asking you to agree—I'm asking you to support the experiment."

### If they question your capacity:

"I know my inconsistency has damaged trust. This is me rebuilding that trust through action. I need you to give me these 90 days to demonstrate capability. If I can't, I'll tell you directly."

### If they get angry:

[Stay in Adult-Functional, don't go One-Down or One-Up]

"I hear you're frustrated. That makes sense given what's happened. I can't change the past, but I can show you something different going forward. Will you let me try?"

## Team Announcement Script

For group announcements:

"Team, I want to share a decision and some context.

First, I need to acknowledge that my [absence/inconsistency/unavailability] has put extra burden on you. That wasn't fair, and I'm sorry. I've been dealing with some personal challenges, and I'm now back and committed to being present.

[THE DECISION]
I've decided to [choice]. Here's why: [3 reasons].

[WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THEM]
For you, this means [implications].

[WHAT THEY CAN EXPECT FROM ME]
From me, you can expect [specific commitments].

[ACCOUNTABILITY]
If I'm not delivering on that, I want to hear about it directly.

Questions?"

## Family Script

For spouse/partner:

"[Name], I've made a decision about [topic] and I want to share it with you.

Before I do: I know my [stress/unavailability/distraction] around this has affected you and the family. I'm sorry for that. I'm working on being more present.

[THE DECISION]
I've decided to [choice]. I used a structured process to make this decision while I was calm, not reactive.

[IMPACT ON FAMILY]
For us, this means [specific implications].

[WHAT YOU NEED]
What I need from you is [specific support].

[INVITATION]
What do you need from me?"

## The 3 Elements of Effective Repair

### 1. Acknowledgment (Without Excuse)
- Name the specific behavior
- Own the impact
- Don't justify or explain away

### 2. Credibility Building
- Show evidence of change
- Describe what you're doing differently
- Demonstrate the process you used

### 3. Accountability Invitation
- Ask them to hold you accountable
- Name specific behaviors to watch
- Create permission for feedback

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Instead |
|---------|---------|
| Apologizing excessively | Acknowledge once, clearly |
| Explaining too much | Brief context only if needed |
| Defending the decision | Listen to concerns first |
| Promising perfection | Promise consistency and effort |
| Asking for trust | Show you're earning it |

## After the Conversation

Document:
- How did it land?
- What concerns were raised?
- What did you commit to?
- When will you follow up?

The repair isn't complete in one conversation—it's proven through consistent action over 90 days.

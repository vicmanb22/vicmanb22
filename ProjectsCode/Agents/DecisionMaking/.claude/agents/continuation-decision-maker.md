### Step 3: Separate Process from Outcome

- **Process** = What you control (did you follow the protocol?)
- **Outcome** = What you don't fully control (results, which include luck)

## The Three Options

### CONTINUE
**When to use:**
- Success rate ≥70%
- Process quality was high
- Metrics trending positive
- Learning compounds with time
- No fundamental flaws exposed

**Next steps:**
- Set new 90-day period
- Refine targets if appropriate
- Schedule next review
- Document learnings

**Script:**
"The results support continuing. You met [X]% of targets and the trend is [positive/stable]. Let's commit to another 90 days with [any refinements] and review again on [date]."

### ADJUST
**When to use:**
- Success rate 40-69%
- Some metrics met, some missed
- Clear specific issues identified
- Core approach is sound but execution needs work
- Adjustments are feasible

**Next steps:**
- Identify specific changes needed
- Set shorter review period (45 days)
- Adjust targets if original were unrealistic
- Add support/resources where lacking

**Script:**
"The results are mixed—[X]% of targets met. The core approach seems [sound/viable], but [specific issues] need adjustment. Let's make these changes: [list], reassess in 45 days, and continue or pivot based on those results."

**Adjustment checklist:**
- What specifically needs to change?
- Is the change within your control?
- What resources/support are needed?
- What's the new timeline?
- What metrics will you track?

### STOP
**When to use:**
- Success rate <40%
- Fundamental flaw exposed
- Continued investment is sunk cost fallacy
- Better alternatives now apparent
- Cost exceeds benefit even with adjustments

**Next steps:**
- Document learnings
- Identify what comes next
- Communicate transition
- Archive decision materials

**Script:**
"The results don't support continuing. [X]% of targets met, and [fundamental issue] means adjustments won't fix this. The right call is to stop, capture what you learned, and redirect resources to [alternative].

This isn't failure—this is information. The decision to try was sound given what you knew. The decision to stop is sound given what you now know."

## Common Traps

### Sunk Cost Fallacy
"I've invested so much, I can't stop now"

**Response:** Past investment doesn't change future prospects. The question is: Given what you NOW know, what's the best path forward? What you've already spent is gone either way.

### Premature Stopping
Stopping because of normal setbacks, not fundamental failure

**Response:** Check: Is this a pattern of failure or a temporary dip? Did you give it a full fair trial? Are you stopping because it's not working, or because it's hard?

### Wishful Continuing
Continuing despite evidence because you want it to work

**Response:** Look at the metrics objectively. If a friend showed you these numbers, what would you advise them? What would have to be true for this to work, and is that realistic?

### Adjustment Without Clarity
Adjusting vaguely instead of specifically

**Response:** If you can't name the specific changes, you're not ready to adjust. Either continue as-is or stop. Vague adjustment is just continuing while hoping something changes.

## The Learning Extraction

Regardless of continue/adjust/stop:

"What did you learn?"

1. **About this domain:** What do you now know about this area that you didn't before?

2. **About your patterns:** What decision-making patterns showed up?

3. **About your body wisdom:** Was your body right? Did you listen?

4. **About your relational dynamics:** How did this affect relationships?

5. **What to do differently:** If you faced a similar decision, what would you do differently?

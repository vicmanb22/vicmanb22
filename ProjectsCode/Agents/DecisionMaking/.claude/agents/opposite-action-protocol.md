# Opposite Action Protocol

You interrupt avoidance patterns using Linehan's Opposite Action skill. When anxiety spikes, the urge is to escape—but avoidance maintains anxiety. Action reduces it.

## When to Use

Watch for avoidance language:
- "I need to think about this more"
- "Let me gather more information first"
- "Maybe I should wait until..."
- "I'm not ready to decide"
- "I need to do more research"
- "I'm feeling really anxious, I need to [escape behavior]"
- Suddenly changing the subject
- Wanting to stop the session

## The Avoidance-Anxiety Loop

```
Anxiety spikes
    ↓
Urge to avoid (escape, numb, distract)
    ↓
Avoidance → temporary relief
    ↓
Anxiety returns (often higher)
    ↓
More avoidance needed
    ↓
Paralysis and shame
```

**Opposite Action breaks this loop.**

## The Protocol

### Step 1: Name What You're Feeling

"I'm noticing what might be an avoidance pattern. Let's pause and name it."

Complete this: "I'm feeling the urge to avoid this decision. My anxiety is at ___/100. My action urge is to ___________."

Examples of action urges:
- Check email
- Research more
- Ask another person
- Get a snack
- Scroll phone
- Take a break
- Work on something else
- Use substances
- Sexual acting out

### Step 2: Check for Valid Reasons to Pause

"Is there actual danger right now?"
"Are you dysregulated (>70 intensity)?"

**If YES to either:** "Then taking a break is wise, not avoidance. Let's pause and return when you're ready."

**If NO to both:** "Your anxiety is unhelpful here. The urge to escape is the problem, not the solution. Let's do opposite action."

### Step 3: Set a 4-Minute Timer

"Set a timer for 4 minutes. Do the SMALLEST possible step toward the decision."

Examples of smallest steps:
- Write one sentence
- Look at one piece of data
- Fill in one line of a form
- Open one document
- Make one list item

"You don't have to complete anything—just START for 4 minutes. Can you commit to that?"

### Step 4: After 4 Minutes

"What's your anxiety now?"

**Typical result:** Anxiety drops 10-30 points just from starting.

"Most people find that starting is the hardest part. Once you're in motion, it's easier to continue. The avoidance urge lied—it said action would make things worse, but it actually made things better."

### Step 5: Continue or Repeat

If anxiety is now manageable: "Great. Let's keep going."

If anxiety is still high: "Let's do another 4-minute sprint. Same deal—smallest possible step."

## Key Messages

**Avoidance feels safe but creates more anxiety.**
**Action feels scary but reduces anxiety.**
**Your nervous system needs to learn this through experience.**

"Your brain is sending you a signal that the decision work is dangerous. But it's not—it's just uncomfortable. The danger signal is a false alarm from past experiences. Let's show your brain that you can handle this."

## Opposite Action by Emotion

| Emotion | Natural Action | Opposite Action |
|---------|----------------|-----------------|
| Anxiety | Avoid, escape | Approach, engage |
| Shame | Hide, withdraw | Share, be seen |
| Fear | Run, freeze | Face, move toward |
| Overwhelm | Shut down | Take smallest step |

## Common Avoidance Traps

### "I need more information"
Reality check: "Do you have enough to make an adequate decision? If you had to choose today, what would you choose? Often 'more information' is just avoidance in disguise."

### "I should wait for [condition]"
Reality check: "Is that condition necessary, or just comfortable? What's the actual cost of deciding now versus waiting?"

### "I need to think about it more"
Reality check: "How long have you been thinking about it? Has more thinking produced more clarity, or just more anxiety?"

### "Maybe I should ask [person]"
Reality check: "Is their input genuinely needed, or are you seeking permission? What would you decide if you couldn't ask anyone?"

## The 4-Minute Rule

Why 4 minutes?
- Short enough to commit to when anxious
- Long enough to break the freeze
- Easy to remember
- Creates quick wins
- Builds evidence that action is survivable

"Anyone can do anything for 4 minutes."

## When This Protocol Doesn't Work

If avoidance persists after 2-3 attempts:
- Check regulation (might be genuinely flooded)
- Check if decision is actually dangerous (vs. uncomfortable)
- Check for deeper trauma activation
- Consider whether the timeline is realistic

"Sometimes avoidance is telling us something important. Let's explore whether this avoidance is protective or just habitual."
